Most people think you can only make big money on Facebook with superficial content about pets, memes, dads, moms, or celebrity drama. They assume serious topics like economics or financial news are "too boring" for the average Facebook user.

But it's really possible to make serious money from the economics and financial news niche on Facebook.

Over the last few years, Facebook has quietly become a strong revenue channel again for publishers and creators, especially through Facebook's Content Monetization program.

At the same time, ad networks like Mediavine and Raptive are paying some of the highest RPMs to sites in niches like personal finance, money, and business.

This combination creates a powerful opportunity - serious, high-value financial and economic content distributed on Facebook, monetized on both Facebook and your own website.

In this case study, we analyze how a top economics and finance news Facebook page structures its strategy, and how you can apply the same framework.

## Why economics & financial news works on Facebook

Most people still think Facebook is just for memes and funny videos. They're wrong.

The platform actively rewards content that's engaging, useful, and keeps people coming back. Even serious topics - like economics or finance can thrive if packaged the right way.

Here is why the economics/financial news niche works so well:

- **High-value audience:** People interested in money, markets, and policy are usually older, higher income, and more valuable to advertisers.
- **Premium RPMs:** Display ad networks show that personal finance and related niches have some of the highest revenue per visitor, with many sites seeing RPMs in the $30-$50+ range in strong quarters.
- **Growing demand for analysis:** Since the global economy changes fast (inflation, interest rates, elections, wars), people want clear, simple explanations more than ever, not just headlines.
- **Facebook traffic recovery:** Recent reporting shows many publishers are seeing a rebound in Facebook referral traffic, along with strong earnings from Facebook's content monetization program.

In short, there is real money on the table if you can package complicated economic and financial stories in a simple, emotionally compelling way.

## Why can't people stop clicking on this page's financial news posts

Let's walk through one example of a Facebook page focused on economics and financial news that is doing very well.

### 1. Clear niche and topical focus

**Josh Rincon's Facebook Page**

This page is not about "everything news." It focuses almost entirely on:

- Major economic news
- Financial markets
- Policy and politics when they affect the economy or people's wallets

This focus builds an audience that knows exactly what to expect - If something big happens in the economy or markets, this page will break it down for me.

### 2. Trending, news-driven content with a financial angle

**Trending topics coverage**

The page doesn't just post random ideas - it follows the news cycle. A story may start as pure politics, tech, or geopolitics, but the page always asks - "What is the financial or economic angle here?"

For example:

- A tech layoff story becomes: **What this layoff wave means for the job market and salaries this year.**
- A war headline becomes: **How this conflict could affect oil prices and your cost of living.**
- An election story becomes: **How this candidate's policy could change taxes, interest rates, or benefits.**

This is how you turn any general news story into a financial news story that regular people care about.

## Post types that perform best

One big strength of this page is that it does not rely on only one post format. It tests and uses the formats Facebook is pushing, and that data shows that it can earn more from Facebook's monetization program.

### 1. Image posts with strong headlines

Photos combined with sharp, curiosity-driven headlines tend to earn the bulk of revenue in Facebook's latest content monetization model for many publishers, because they drive high engagement.

Examples:

- Clear image (chart, politician, company logo, money)
- Big, bold, emotional headline, e.g., **New Law Could Quietly Cut Your Retirement By 20%.**
- Short descriptive caption that offers context, then a question or hook at the end

These posts are simple to produce and can be used multiple times per day.

### 2. Viral text posts - especially with black backgrounds

**Text post with black background**

Facebook's colored background text posts are still incredibly effective for grabbing attention in a crowded feed. Among these, simple, high-contrast designs such as black backgrounds with white text often perform best because they stand out visually.

Typical use cases:

- Strong opinion on a hot topic: **Raise your hand if you think central banks have lost control.**
- Simple polls or yes/no questions: **Would you support a law that caps rent increases at 2% per year?**
- Short shock stats: **A 1% rise in interest rates can add thousands to your mortgage over the loan term. Are you worried?**

These posts are cheap and quick to create and can drive high engagement, boosting overall page performance.

### 3. Simple political posts with an economic angle

**Politics + finance**

The page also "dabbles" in political content, but not as generic outrage. It ties politics back to people's wallets.

For example:

- This candidate wants to raise the capital gains tax from X% to Y%. How would that affect you?
- New budget proposal – who wins, who loses?

Political content can be risky, but when grounded in economics and explained in simple, neutral language, it can drive a lot of comments and shares.

### 4. Experimental engagement-bait posts

**Experiments**

The page tests some "engagement bait" style posts too - questions, "comment your country," or simple opinion prompts. Not all of them work, and some may underperform or feel off-brand.

Two important points here:

- Testing is good, but do it wisely.
- It is usually better to test experimental formats on smaller test pages so you don't drag down your main page's quality signals.

Facebook does pay based on content performance and various signals, so you want your main page to look as strong as possible.

## How he turns complex topics into viral posts

**Simple information in a sensational way**

One of the most impressive things this page does is take topics you would never expect to do well on Facebook - like bond yields, monetary policy, or obscure macroeconomic data and make them perform by "packaging" them correctly.

Here is the basic method:

1. **Start with a complex event**
   - Example: A central bank raises interest rates.
2. **Translate into a simple, emotional impact**
   - Your mortgage and loan payments are about to get more expensive.
3. **Add a sensational but honest framing**
   - Central bank just pulled a brutal move on borrowers.
4. **Use a strong visual or background**
   - Black background text, image of a bank, or a chart showing the rate jump.
5. **Ask a question that invites opinion**
   - Do you think this is fair?
   - Is it time to fix how interest rates are set?

This is where you, as a creator/publisher, add real value - you are not inventing news, you are translating it into something the average person can quickly understand and react to.

If you run a Facebook page like this and you are not building a site, you are leaving a lot of money and long-term stability on the table.

## How to turn this into a full system - step-by-step

Here is how you could model this approach as a Facebook publisher with long-term, authority-driven ambitions.

### Step 1: Define your economic/financial niche

Pick a clear lane so people instantly "get" your page:

- Macro and economic news for normal people
- Personal finance and cost of living
- Markets and investing for beginners
- Business and policy that affect jobs and wages

Your positioning example: **"Simple explanations of money, markets, and policy so you understand what is happening to your wallet"**

### Step 2: Build a content engine around the news cycle

Create daily or weekly routines:

- Monitor 10+ trusted sources of financial and economic news curation
- Each day, pick 3-6 stories that can be translated into - what this means for you and your money
- Remember, your job is not to break the news first but to explain it best and fastest in simple language.

### Step 3: Use proven post formats and test variations

Rely on a mix of:

- Image posts with clear, emotional headlines
- Background text posts - especially high-contrast, use black backgrounds
- Occasional political-economic takes that invite discussion

Track what performs best in terms of:

- Reach
- Comments and shares
- RPM / earnings (if you have Content Monetization access)

Over time, shift more effort into the post types and angles that consistently perform.

### Step 4: Monetize on Facebook with Facebook's programs

Many publishers are seeing six- and even seven-figure annual revenue from Facebook's current content monetization program, especially those posting a lot of photos and text content with strong engagement.

Key actions:

- Make sure your page meets all Facebook eligibility criteria.
- Focus heavily on posts that drive interactions, because the program rewards engagement-driven content.
- Encourage genuine discussion: ask questions, share conflicting viewpoints, run simple polls, and reply to comments on the page to keep threads active.

Remember, one exec in a recent report said they were on track for seven figures from this program, with photos driving most of the revenue.

### Step 5: Build and monetize your own website

Once your page has steady traffic and loyal followers, you should build a site as soon as possible.

Basic roadmap:

- **Launch a clean, fast site:**
  - Sections for daily news breakdowns, explainers, and guides.
- **Apply to a premium ad network** when you hit the traffic thresholds (e.g., Mediavine or Raptive), which often deliver strong RPMs in finance-related niches.
- **Repurpose your best performing posts into short articles:**
  - A viral Facebook post about an interest rate hike becomes a 1,000-word explainer.
- **Link back and forth:**
  - Facebook posts send traffic to website articles.
  - Articles invite readers to follow your Facebook page and join your email list.

This creates a flywheel where Facebook gives you reach and traffic, your site gives you stable ad revenue and email list growth, and both together grow your authority.
